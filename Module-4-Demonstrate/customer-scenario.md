# Customer Scenario: Kenstrel Health Alliance

## Overview

Kenstrel Health Alliance is a rapidly growing regional healthcare organization operating 8 outpatient clinics across multiple communities.

The organization provides a broad range of patient services, including routine consultations, specialty referrals, follow-up care, preventative screenings, and appointment scheduling.

As patient volume has increased, administrative staff now generate and send more than 1,500 patient communications every week.

These communications include:

- Appointment confirmations
- Follow-up reminders
- Scheduling updates
- Pre-visit instructions
- Referral coordination messages
- General patient outreach

---

## Current Situation

Most communications are manually drafted by administrative staff.

Employees frequently copy information from scheduling systems and patient records into emails before sending them to patients.

Because healthcare organizations must follow HIPAA Safe Harbor regulations, staff are responsible for ensuring that Protected Health Information (PHI) is not accidentally exposed through inappropriate communication channels.

Examples of potentially sensitive information include:

- Patient names
- Dates associated with medical services
- Clinical specialties
- Medical conditions
- Treatment references
- Patient identifiers

The responsibility to manually review every communication has created significant operational friction.

---

## Business Problem

Administrative employees are spending excessive time reviewing routine communications before sending them.

The review process is inconsistent and depends heavily on individual judgment.

As message volume increases, leadership is concerned that:

- Compliance risk is increasing
- Operational efficiency is decreasing
- Employee confidence is declining
- Human error remains unavoidable

The organization needs a better way to reduce compliance risk without slowing down day-to-day operations.

---

## Stakeholders

### Executive Leadership

Executive leaders are concerned about:

- Regulatory exposure
- Compliance violations
- Reputational risk
- Operational scalability

They need confidence that patient communications can be handled safely as the organization grows.

### Administrative Staff

Administrative employees want:

- Faster communication workflows
- Clear guidance
- Reduced fear of making mistakes
- Minimal additional steps

They need a solution that supports their work rather than creating additional complexity.

### Compliance Teams

Compliance teams need:

- Consistent communication practices
- Reduced likelihood of PHI exposure
- Clear enforcement of policy boundaries
- Human review mechanisms when necessary

---

## Desired Outcome

Kenstrel Health Alliance wants to explore whether a lightweight AI-assisted workflow could:

- Identify potential PHI before messages are sent
- Reduce manual review effort
- Improve consistency
- Maintain human oversight
- Demonstrate a safer future-state workflow

Leadership is not looking for a production-ready system.

At this stage, they want to see a rapid prototype that answers a simple question:

> Could a lightweight compliance assistant reduce risk and administrative effort without requiring a major infrastructure investment?

---

## Rapid Prototyping Challenge

Design and demonstrate a possible solution that helps staff identify potentially sensitive information in patient communications before sending them.

The prototype should:

- Focus on workflow validation
- Use synthetic sample data only
- Remain simple and visual
- Be understandable in less than 30 seconds
- Support business discussion and feedback

The prototype is intended to demonstrate an idea, not deliver a production-ready healthcare platform.

---

## Success Criteria

The prototype will be considered successful if stakeholders can:

1. Quickly understand the proposed workflow.
2. See how risk could be reduced.
3. Visualize a future-state process.
4. Provide meaningful feedback.
5. Decide whether the idea is worth exploring further.

---

## Key Question

How might Kenstrel Health Alliance help staff detect potential PHI in patient communications before sending them, while maintaining speed, simplicity, and human oversight?
